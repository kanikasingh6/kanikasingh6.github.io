The Challenge

Frontline Insights With No Path to Impact

Amazon India operations had a significant untapped resource: thousands of associates with direct operational experience identifying inefficiencies, workarounds, and improvement ideas daily. Associates had submission rights under the AIS programme, but there was no structured mechanism to help those ideas be framed, prioritised, and successfully implemented at scale.

Ideas were being submitted, but without coaching, governance, or dedicated support, most never made it through review. The programme existed, but lacked the infrastructure to convert frontline insight into approved, implemented improvements. Ideas that could have saved hundreds of hours or millions of dollars were being lost in the process.

The challenge was not just maintaining a submission pipeline, but building the governance, coaching, and culture to make that pipeline work at high quality,with a meaningful implementation rate,and real accountability for follow-through.

My Role

AIS Programme Lead, India Operations

As AIS Programme Lead, I owned the full innovation lifecycle for India Operations: from programme design and access enablement, through idea intake and governance reviews, to implementation tracking and impact reporting.

I was also the primary coach for associates and team leads — running structured sessions to help teams frame ideas correctly, quantify impact credibly, and navigate the approval process successfully.

Approach

Building Innovation Infrastructure

1

Access Enablement

Secured AIS submission rights for India associates for the first time, working with regional leadership and the global AIS team to expand programme access. Established the governance framework, roles, and responsibilities for the India programme.

2

Intake & Governance Design

Designed the intake process including submission templates, idea quality criteria, and review cadences. Built a structured governance model with defined stages: submission, initial review, detailed evaluation, approval, and implementation tracking.

3

Coaching Programme

Created and delivered coaching sessions teaching associates how to frame operational problems clearly, quantify business impact credibly, and structure proposals that would succeed in review. Managed 115+ idea submissions per half-year at high quality.

4

Open Office Hours

Launched a dedicated Open Office Hours programme (58 participants) giving associates direct access to support for developing their submissions. This significantly increased both the volume and quality of ideas reaching the review stage.

5

Implementation & Impact Tracking

Tracked approved ideas through to implementation, coordinating with Operations, Engineering, and Finance to ensure changes were delivered and impact was measured. Built reporting for senior leadership on pipeline health and cumulative savings.

Key Learnings

What This Programme Taught Me

Governance enables scale

Without clear intake criteria, review stages, and decision rights, a high-volume pipeline becomes a backlog. Good governance is what allows an innovation programme to run at 115 ideas per cycle without losing quality.

Coaching is the multiplier

The ideas were always there. What was missing was the skill to frame them. Teaching associates to quantify and structure their ideas turned good observations into approvable business cases.

Access is not enough

Giving India associates submission rights was necessary but not sufficient. The investment in coaching, open office hours, and ongoing support was what drove the ~95% approval rate once ideas reached review.
