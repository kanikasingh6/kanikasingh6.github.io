The Challenge

A Silent, Structural Revenue Leak

Amazon's FBA false overage claim process operated within a rigid 30/45-day date range window. This constraint meant that a significant volume of legitimate overages went unreconciled — not because they were invalid, but because the system couldn't account for them in time.

The two separate CTIs handling claims (IAR and WHL) had misaligned date ranges, creating inconsistency, confusion among associates, and persistent missed recovery opportunities. Millions of dollars in reconcilable overages were aging out of the system every month, with no structured path to recover them.

The problem wasn't visible at leadership level. It was a frontline insight that needed someone willing to identify it, quantify it, and drive it all the way to implementation across six global teams.

My Role

AIS Programme Lead & Project Manager

This was not a pre-assigned project. I identified the opportunity independently, built the business case from scratch, drove all stakeholder engagement, managed the cross-team approval process, and owned the implementation through to completion.

I quantified the financial impact, aligned multi-stakeholder groups across regions, managed the technical implementation with tool and engineering teams, and presented outcomes to senior leadership in Weekly Business Reviews.

Approach

A Five-Stage End-to-End Programme

1

Problem Discovery & Framing

Identified the constraint during routine analysis of false overage SIMs. Recognised that expanding the claim window from 30/45 to 45/60 days would align date ranges across both CTIs and unlock a large volume of unreconciled claims.

2

Impact Quantification

Built a data-driven business case: estimated ~$383,500/month in additional savings from expanded claim opportunities. Modelled downstream impact on unreconciled SIM volume and claim success rates across both CTIs using SQL and internal dashboards.

3

Stakeholder Navigation

Submitted through the Amazon Innovation Submission (AIS) process. Managed multiple review cycles, provided additional data sets on request, addressed pushbacks, and maintained the proposal through a complex multi-team approval chain spanning Operations, Finance, and Engineering.

4

Cross-Functional Coordination

Partnered across Operations, Finance, Engineering, and regional leadership across India, EU, and the US to align on change scope, implementation approach, and rollout plan. Managed competing priorities and kept the programme moving despite delays and stakeholder churn.

5

Programme Rollout & Adoption

Led end-to-end delivery across 6 operational teams. Managed change communications, updated SOPs, trained associates on new claim windows, and tracked post-implementation metrics to confirm impact realisation.

Key Learnings

What This Programme Taught Me

Ownership is the difference

No one asked me to find this problem. No one assigned this project. The willingness to pick it up and drive it through multiple rounds of pushback is what created $4.5M in impact.

Quantification unlocks approval

A good idea without data is a suggestion. A good idea with a credible financial model is a business case. Learning to model impact rigorously was the key to getting six global teams aligned.

Patience is a structured discipline

This programme touched three continents and required 18 months of alignment. Patience, in the operational sense, means persistent and structured follow-through, not waiting.

Stakeholder management is a skill, not a task

Each gatekeeper in a large organisation needs something different to feel confident saying yes. Understanding what that is, and providing exactly that, is the real work of programme management.
